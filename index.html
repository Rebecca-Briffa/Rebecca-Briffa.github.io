I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at the Institute of Space Sciences and Astronomy, University of Malta.

In my postdoc I am building machine-learning pipelines based on genetic algorithms to reconstruct dark-energy and cosmological models from data. Instead of fixing one functional form in advance, the algorithm searches a wide space of possible expansions of the Universe; a way to test physics beyond ΛCDM while cosmological tensions, especially the Hubble tension, remain unresolved. This is the main thread of my current work, and part of a wider cosmological effort on cosmological observational tensions.

My Ph.D. in cosmology began with gravity beyond General Relativity, in particular teleparallel gravity and f(T) models, tested against late-time data: supernovae (including Pantheon+), cosmic chronometers, baryon acoustic oscillations, and redshift-space distortions, using Gaussian processes and MCMC. That work has now grown into a full Boltzmann pipeline: we have modified CLASS so teleparallel f(T) can be run end to end. We now have not only late-time expansion results, but also an early-Universe test of the theory using CMB data, including Planck and high-resolution measurements from SPT and ACT.

I completed a B.Sc. (Hons) in Physics and Mathematics at the University of Malta, then a Ph.D. in cosmology (2024), supervised by Prof. Jackson Levi Said.

Outside of academia I am, unsurprisingly for someone from an island, happiest with the sea and the sun. I love a good book or a good film, and I am finding my way back to a long-lost love: running.

Highlighted work

  • Genetic algorithms for dark energy beyond ΛCDM

    Ongoing postdoctoral project: a data-driven genetic-algorithm pipeline that reconstructs dark-energy behaviour and explores models beyond ΛCDM in the presence of cosmological tensions.

  • A full f(T) pipeline in CLASS

    We have modified CLASS into a complete f(T) pipeline, with late-time expansion results and an early-Universe test using CMB data from Planck, SPT, and ACT.

  • Constraints on f(T) cosmology with Pantheon+

    Used the Pantheon+ supernova sample to test teleparallel f(T) models against late-time expansion data.

  • Growth of structures using redshift-space distortions in f(T) cosmology

    Combined expansion-history data with large-scale structure growth to see whether modified teleparallel gravity can match how cosmic structure forms.

  • Impact of H0 priors on f(T) late-time cosmology

    Showed how the choice of Hubble-constant prior changes the constraints on f(T) models — part of understanding tensions in modified gravity.
